# Editorial policy

Awesome Study Tools is a selective directory, not a leaderboard, endorsement program, or complete inventory of education software.

## Audience and scope

The primary audience is students, educators, and families choosing tools that support learning. We include products and open resources for remembering, understanding, practising, researching, writing, planning, and learning with others. Institutional products may be listed when students are likely to encounter them through a school or university.

Regional inclusion means a tool is shaped by a local curriculum, examination system, language, access constraint, or study culture. It does not mean the tool is unavailable elsewhere, and a regional section never claims complete coverage.

## Selection

A listing must have a working canonical page, a clear learning function, and enough evidence of ongoing value to justify maintenance. Longevity, open access, distinctive pedagogy, meaningful community use, and underserved regional needs all count as editorial merit.

We exclude affiliate and referral links, paid placement, generic tools without a concrete learning use, misleading or unsafe services, and products that cannot be verified as available. New launches may be deferred until their usefulness and continuity can be assessed.

## Descriptions and evidence

Descriptions are short, factual, and capability-based. The catalog deliberately omits exact prices, user counts, valuations, funding, pass rates, and superlatives because they become stale quickly and are often difficult to compare fairly.

Canonical provider pages are adequate evidence for basic capabilities. Lifecycle, ownership, safety, and comparative claims need a first-party announcement or reputable independent source. Editors record the review date but never imply that every product was exhaustively tested.

Automated checks may detect broken links or stale records, but they do not change facts or product status without human review.

## Ordering and classification

Entries are alphabetical within each generated view. Position conveys no ranking.

- `native`: AI is central to the product's main learning experience.
- `features`: AI augments a product whose primary experience also works without it.
- `none`: no substantial AI feature was identified at the review date.

Access labels are broad models, not price promises. `Free` means the principal listed experience is free; `Freemium` combines a meaningful free tier with paid upgrades; `Paid` requires purchase or subscription; `Pay as you go` charges by lesson, course, or use; `Institutional` is normally provided through an organization.

## Conflicts of interest

Maintainer-affiliated products are marked 🏠 in every rendered view and receive no placement advantage. Contributors must disclose employment, ownership, investment, advisory, referral, or other material relationships.

Changes that add or materially expand a maintainer-affiliated listing should be reviewed by a second person who is not the change author whenever another maintainer is available. Until then, the change must include public evidence and an explicit affiliation note. Promotional wording is rejected regardless of affiliation.

## Corrections, removals, and appeals

Broken links, rebrands, and factual errors should be corrected promptly. A tool may be removed from the active catalog when it is discontinued, persistently unreachable, deceptive, unsafe, or no longer within scope. Historical records and the evidence used for removal may be retained in `ARCHIVE.md`.

Anyone may challenge an editorial decision in a public issue. Maintainers will explain the applicable criterion, correct genuine errors, and record material policy changes in the repository history.

## Review cadence

Automated validation runs on every change, external links are checked on a schedule, and maintainers review flagged records. Dates describe the last editorial pass, not a guarantee of current pricing, availability, privacy, or accuracy.
